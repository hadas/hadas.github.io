Hadas Raviv

Studying Language, Learning, and Intelligence

I am a Research Scholar at the Princeton Neuroscience Institute, working with Prof. Uri Hasson. I am interested in how language is learned from human naturalistic experience and how it is represented in the human mind.

Language has been the common thread throughout my research - from information retrieval and NLP to my current work on language learning. I am fascinated by language because it is a primary way for us to represent the world and also shape and change it. While our experience is continuous and multidimensional, language allows us to discretize it, direct attention toward particular aspects, generate new thoughts by combining representations, and communicate them to others.

Despite their remarkable language capabilities, large language models and modern AI systems operate at scales and under training conditions that differ sharply from a child’s natural experience. This motivates my use of AI to investigate how language can be learned under more human-like conditions. My work combines cognitively motivated training protocols with ultra-dense, real-world data that capture learning environments and evaluation methods that reflect human learning under realistic developmental timescales. I am currently focused on early lexical and syntactic learning, particularly how children acquire language through everyday speech.
